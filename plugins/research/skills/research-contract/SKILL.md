# Research Contract

Every retriever produces **claims** as JSON; the citation-verifier aggregates
and existence-checks them; the evidence-adjudicator entailment-checks the
survivors; the result is one JSON report. **JSON is canonical; Markdown is
_rendered_ from it** by `schema.py` (beside this file, stdlib-only;
`python3 schema.py --schema` prints the field-by-field shape — the
machine-enforceable half of this contract). Agents never hand-write the
Markdown report. A claim that omits a resolvable `identifier` **and** a
verbatim `quote` **fails validation and is never rendered** — every claim a
human reads carries a source they can open and a sentence they can check.

## The Iron Law

> NO SOURCE = NO CLAIM. EXISTENCE IS NOT SUPPORT. EVERY GROUNDED CLAIM
> CARRIES A RESOLVABLE IDENTIFIER, A VERBATIM QUOTE, AND AN EVIDENCE GRADE.

Three failures this law forbids, in order of how often they happen:

1. **Claim with no source.** The model wrote a sentence from its own memory.
   Forbidden: retrieve first, cite only what you retrieved.
2. **Source that exists but does not support the claim.** A real PMID is
   attached to a sentence the paper never makes. This is the *dominant*
   residual error in even the best citation systems — a verified citation
   proves the paper exists, not that it backs the sentence. Defeated only by a
   separate entailment gate (below).
3. **Claim graded by feeling, not evidence.** A tier or GRADE level assigned
   from fluency rather than study design and replication. Anchor every grade
   to the concrete criteria in `evidence-grading` (preloaded reference).

Abstention beats fabrication. If no retrieved passage supports a claim,
emit **"Insufficient evidence"** rather than the claim. A short report of
verified claims outperforms a long report of plausible ones.

## Two Modes

The pipeline runs in one of two modes, set at triage:

- **grounded** (default) — only claims tethered to retrieved sources, graded
  and tiered. No hypotheses.
- **hypothesis** — grounded claims *plus* a fenced theorizing channel
  (`hypotheses[]`) that proposes mechanisms beyond the literature. Hypotheses
  are generated only by combining cited facts (literature-based discovery),
  never by inventing facts, and every hypothesis ships with a falsification
  test. The wall between grounded claims and hypotheses is absolute: a
  hypothesis never renders as a grounded claim and vice-versa.

## Evidence Tiers

One tier per grounded claim, visible inline, anchored to **concrete
criteria** (independent-source count, study design, replication, directness)
— never to the model's felt confidence. The full grading procedure is in the
preloaded `evidence-grading` reference; this is the closed set the schema
enforces:

| Tier | ≈ GRADE | Criteria | Informative verb |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **ESTABLISHED** | High | Multiple low-bias RCTs / strong systematic reviews; guideline-endorsed | "X reduces / results in …" |
| **EMERGING** | Moderate | Consistent but limited; some bias or few studies | "X likely / probably results in …" |
| **CONTESTED** | n/a | Genuine expert disagreement / competing paradigms (long COVID, ME/CFS, chronic Lyme) — present **all** sides with each side's grade; do not adjudicate beyond the evidence | "evidence is divided on whether X …" |
| **SPECULATIVE** | Low / Very Low | Single small study, preclinical, mechanistic reasoning, **or a hypothesis generated by this skill** | "X may result in …" + an explicit *hypothesis, not a finding* flag |

Reserve hedging for genuine uncertainty: over-hedging buries ESTABLISHED
findings and is as misleading as over-claiming.

## OCEBM Level + GRADE Certainty

Every grounded claim carries two grades (computed per `evidence-grading`):

- **`ocebm_level`** — fast per-citation study-design tag: `"1"` (SR of RCTs)
  · `"2"` (individual RCT) · `"3"` (non-randomized cohort) · `"4"`
  (case-series / case-control) · `"5"` (mechanism-based reasoning). `null`
  only for a hypothesis.
- **`grade`** — `HIGH | MODERATE | LOW | VERY_LOW` certainty for the body of
  evidence behind the claim. Start RCT = High, observational = Low; rate down
  for risk-of-bias / inconsistency / indirectness / imprecision /
  publication-bias; rate observational up for large effect / dose-response /
  confounders-would-reduce-effect.

## Citation Schema

Every citation is a JSON object (enforced by `schema.py`):

```json
{
  "identifier": "PMID:34308300",
  "url": "https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34308300/",
  "quote": "verbatim supporting sentence copied from the source",
  "locator": "Results, paragraph 3",
  "source_type": "systematic-review | rct | cohort | case-series | preprint | trial-registry | guideline | patient-led | mechanism | other",
  "peer_reviewed": true,
  "retracted": false
}
```

- **`identifier`** — `PMID:<n>`, `DOI:<doi>`, `PMCID:<id>`, or `NCT:<id>`.
  Required. The verifier resolves it (Gate 1); an unresolvable identifier
  kills the citation.
- **`url`** — deterministic resolver: DOI → `https://doi.org/<doi>`, PMID →
  `https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/<n>/`, NCT →
  `https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/<id>`. Required.
- **`quote`** — the verbatim sentence that supports the claim, copied from the
  retrieved text. Required. A claim with no locatable quote is not a grounded
  claim. **Never paraphrase into the quote field.**
- **`locator`** — section/paragraph/page. Strongly preferred; it is how a
  human (and Gate 2) finds the quote.
- **`source_type`** — drives `ocebm_level` and tiering.
- **`retracted`** — set `true` if Crossref/PubMed flags a retraction; a
  retracted source down-tiers the claim and must be surfaced, never silently
  dropped.

## Claim Schema

Every grounded claim is a JSON object (enforced by `schema.py`):

```json
{
  "statement": "one-sentence claim, phrased with the tier's informative verb",
  "tier": "ESTABLISHED | EMERGING | CONTESTED | SPECULATIVE",
  "grade": "HIGH | MODERATE | LOW | VERY_LOW",
  "ocebm_level": "1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | null",
  "sub_question": "which planned sub-question this answers",
  "citations": [ Citation, ... ],
  "support": "ENTAILED | PARTIAL | UNSUPPORTED",
  "confidence": 88,
  "case_definition": "IOM 2015 (PEM required)",
  "commercial_conflict": "none",
  "note": "one-line justification or key limitation"
}
```

### Required Fields

`statement`, `tier`, `grade`, `sub_question`, `citations`, `support`, and
`confidence` are required. `citations` must be **non-empty**, and every
citation must carry a resolvable `identifier` and a non-empty `quote` — there
is no grounded claim without a source and a quote.

- **`support`** — the Gate-2 entailment verdict (see Verification). A claim
  reaches the final report only as `ENTAILED` or, downgraded, `PARTIAL`;
  `UNSUPPORTED` claims are dropped by the adjudicator.
- **`case_definition`** — for contested-disease claims, the cohort's case
  definition and whether it requires post-exertional malaise (CCC/ICC/IOM >
  Fukuda > Oxford). Optional elsewhere.
- **`commercial_conflict`** — `"none"` or who profits from the intervention
  being evaluated. Required-in-spirit for any claim about a sold therapy.
- **`confidence`** — integer 0–100 (rubric below).

### Confidence Rubric

Confidence is **the probability that a clinician-researcher who opens the
cited source will agree the claim is supported at the stated grade** — not
your certainty about the topic. Grade it after Gate 2.

- `90-100`: ≥2 independent sources, quote directly entails the claim, grade
  matches study design, falsification of the entailment attempted and failed.
- `80-89`: strong single source or two partial sources; one small assumption
  about scope you could not fully close.
- `70-79`: plausible and worth surfacing, but the quote only partially entails
  the claim (→ mark `support: PARTIAL` and down-tier).
- `<70`: do not emit as a grounded claim — keep searching or abstain.

## Hypothesis Schema (hypothesis mode only)

A hypothesis lives in `hypotheses[]`, never in `claims[]`. It is generated by
**literature-based discovery**: take a cited A→B relation from one source and
a cited B→C relation from another, and propose **A→C** — the novelty is the
*connection*, the edges stay cited. Full method in the preloaded `theorizing`
reference.

```json
{
  "statement": "A may contribute to C, via B",
  "chain": [
    {"from": "A", "relation": "elevates", "to": "B", "citation": 0},
    {"from": "B", "relation": "drives",   "to": "C", "citation": 1}
  ],
  "citations": [ Citation, ... ],
  "tier": "SPECULATIVE",
  "novelty": "novel | established",
  "assumptions": ["each leap the chain takes that the sources do not state"],
  "competing": ["≥1 alternative mechanism that explains the same observation"],
  "discriminating_test": "the experiment/observation that would separate this from the competitors",
  "falsification": "the specific result that would refute this hypothesis",
  "confidence": 35
}
```

### Required Fields

`statement`, `chain`, `citations`, `tier` (always `SPECULATIVE`),
`discriminating_test`, and `falsification` are required. Every `chain` edge
must reference a citation index whose source asserts that edge — an edge with
no citation is a fabricated fact and fails validation. `competing` must hold
≥1 alternative (Strong Inference: never advance a single pet mechanism).
`novelty: "novel"` is permitted only after the novelty check confirms the A→C
connection is absent from the retrieved sources; otherwise it is
`"established"` and belongs as a grounded claim.

## Verification — Two Independent Gates

The anti-hallucination core. Existence and support are *separate* checks
because a real citation routinely fails to support its claim.

- **Gate 1 — Existence (citation-verifier).** Resolve every `identifier`
  against PubMed/Europe PMC/Crossref/ClinicalTrials.gov. Reject any that does
  not resolve. Pull retraction status; mark retracted sources. Deduplicate
  claims by casefolded DOI + lowercased statement.
- **Gate 2 — Support / entailment (evidence-adjudicator).** For every
  ESTABLISHED/EMERGING/CONTESTED claim, re-fetch the source and check the
  `quote` actually entails the `statement`, **with the draft out of context**.
  Set `support`: `ENTAILED` (keep), `PARTIAL` (down-tier one level, keep),
  `UNSUPPORTED` (drop, record in the report). Flag real-but-irrelevant
  citations — they are the dominant failure mode.

The adjudicator **never adds claims, never raises a tier, never invents a
citation**. Recall belongs to the retrievers; the gates buy precision. Expect
to drop or downgrade some claims on every run.

## Safety Rails (non-negotiable)

Enforced on every run regardless of mode; full list in the preloaded
`safety-rails` reference. The renderer requires a non-empty `disclaimer`, and
these rules bind every agent:

- **Persistent framing**: "For research and education; not medical advice; not
  a substitute for a licensed clinician." Re-imposed every run — do not assume
  the base model adds it.
- **No individualized advice**: no diagnosis, dosing, "you should try X", or
  emergency triage. Everything stays population/literature-level and
  conditional.
- **Flag known harm, never neutrally relay it**: graded exercise therapy is
  not presented as recommended (contraindicated since NICE 2021); apheresis /
  "blood-washing", stem-cell/exosome clinics, ozone, and brain-retraining
  programs are surfaced with their documented harms and commercial incentive
  in `safety_flags`, never as options.
- **Anti-false-hope**: on SPECULATIVE/CONTESTED material, avoid efficacy
  language and surface cost and opportunity cost.

## What Not to Report

- **A claim from model memory** — if you did not retrieve it, you cannot cite
  it. Drop it or go retrieve it.
- **A real citation that does not entail the claim** — Gate 2 territory; mark
  `UNSUPPORTED` and drop.
- **A hypothesis dressed as a finding** — speculation in `claims[]` instead of
  `hypotheses[]`. The wall is absolute.
- **A grade asserted without criteria** — if you cannot name the study design
  and replication behind a tier, you have not graded it.
- **An intervention recommendation** — the skill researches; it does not
  prescribe.

## Per-Retriever Report (JSON)

Each retriever **writes exactly one JSON object** to the path the dispatcher
provides (`.research/<timestamp>/NN_<slug>.json`) using `Write`, then returns
a one-line summary (sub-question, claim count by tier, path written) — never
the full JSON, never Markdown. The command validates and renders it with
`python3 schema.py notes`.

```json
{
  "sub_question": "the planned sub-question this retriever owned",
  "question": "the overall research question",
  "date": "YYYY-MM-DD",
  "claims": [],
  "summary": "2-3 sentence retriever-scoped verdict: what was searched, the strongest evidence found, and where evidence was thin or absent."
}
```

`claims` holds Claim objects; use `[]` when the search found nothing citable
(a valid, successful result — never pad).

## Final Research Report (JSON)

The evidence-adjudicator returns **exactly one JSON object and nothing else**
— a single fenced `json` block. The command writes it to
`.research/<timestamp>/report.json` and renders `report.md` with
`python3 schema.py report`.

```json
{
  "question": "the research question",
  "date": "YYYY-MM-DD",
  "author": "<git user name>",
  "mode": "grounded | hypothesis",
  "disclaimer": "For research and education; not medical advice; not a substitute for a licensed clinician.",
  "executive_summary": "200-300 words; each headline finding tagged with its tier.",
  "claims": [],
  "hypotheses": [],
  "contested": [
    {"topic": "...", "positions": [{"stance": "...", "grade": "MODERATE", "note": "..."}]}
  ],
  "safety_flags": [
    {"intervention": "graded exercise therapy", "harm": "post-exertional symptom exacerbation", "commercial": "n/a", "note": "contraindicated for ME/CFS since NICE NG206 (2021)"}
  ],
  "open_questions": ["what could not be resolved and why (incl. paywalled-abstract-only items)"],
  "audit": {"sub_questions": 5, "searches": 42, "claims_dropped": 3, "refusal_rate": "low/medium/high", "caveats": ["API/version caveats, freshness limits"]}
}
```

`hypotheses` is `[]` in grounded mode. Every claim's `citations`,
`identifier`, and `quote` are preserved **verbatim** from the retriever — no
gate ever collapses a citation.

## Rendered Output Layout

The command persists every run under a git-ignored folder:

```
.research/<timestamp>/
  plan.md                      # sub-questions + mode, written by the lead
  NN_<slug>.json / .md         # one per retriever (per sub-question)
  merged.json                  # citation-verifier output (post Gate 1)
  report.json                  # canonical final report (adjudicator output, post Gate 2)
  report.md                    # headline human-readable report
  claim-ledger.json            # per-claim audit trail (statement, identifier, quote, gate verdicts, tier)
.research/latest -> <timestamp>
```

`report.md` is the headline artifact; the per-retriever files preserve each
sub-question's full claim set.

## Filtering Rules (citation-verifier)

Drop claims that:

- Lack a resolvable `identifier` or a non-empty `quote` (schema validation
  also enforces this — the "no claim without source" rule).
- Fail Gate 1 (identifier does not resolve).
- Are duplicates of a higher-graded claim for the same statement.
- Are SPECULATIVE but sitting in `claims[]` — move to `hypotheses[]` or drop.

Do **not** drop CONTESTED claims for disagreeing with each other — present all
sides. Never drop a `safety_flags` entry.

The final bar for every surviving claim: **would a clinician-researcher, on
opening the cited source, agree the statement is supported at the stated tier?**
When in doubt, down-tier or abstain.
